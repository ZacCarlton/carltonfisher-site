Verification and Validation

Verification checks that a system was built the way it was specified. Validation checks that it does the job it was bought to do. We plan the checks, run the tests, tie every requirement to a test, and report what we found in plain terms, sorted by risk.

Marcus Fisher wrote Software Verification and Validation (Springer, 2007) and has done this work on NASA missions.

For systems someone else built, we provide independent verification and validation (IV&V). We do not verify a system we built.

When to call us

  • A state or federal contract requires IV&V.
  • A system goes live soon and you need to know it is ready.
  • A vendor says testing is done and you want a second look.
  • Safety, money or compliance depends on the software working.

What you get

  • A V&V plan written for your system.
  • A trace from each requirement to the test that checks it.
  • Test results.
  • A findings report sorted by risk, with what to fix first.
  • A readiness statement before go-live, and evidence you can hand an auditor.

How it runs

  1. First call.
  2. We read the requirements and the contract and tell you what level of checking fits.
  3. Written plan and estimate with a ceiling.
  4. Testing and analysis, with findings reported as we find them, not saved for the end.
  5. Final report and a walk-through with your team.

Questions

What does "independent" mean here?

The people checking the system are separate from the people who built it, in management, in money and in technical direction. That is how IEEE 1012, the standard for this work, defines it.

Can you check a system you built for us?

No. We will help you find someone who can.

Do you work to a standard?

IEEE 1012, plus your contract's own requirements. We also work to DO-178B/C, ISO 26262, IEC 60880 for nuclear, the NIST Risk Management Framework and Cybersecurity Framework, and the NIST SP 800 series.
